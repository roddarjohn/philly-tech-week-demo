# Executive Summary

## Tech Week Consortium Response to RFP Project Intake #1140
### Special Event Permitting Software Solution — City of Philadelphia, Office of City Representative and Special Events

---

## Philadelphia's Moment. Built by Philadelphia.

The City of Philadelphia is preparing to welcome the world. With FIFA World Cup matches beginning in June 2026 and the MLB All-Star Game following in July, the Office of City Representative and Special Events faces an unmistakable imperative: the 8-page PDF email workflow that currently manages 1,700+ annual permit applications cannot carry the load of what is coming. **Tech Week Consortium (TWC) proposes to replace it — on time, on budget, and built to last well beyond 2026.**

We are not a vendor parachuting in from another city. We are a Philadelphia-headquartered, worker-owned civic technology cooperative, incorporated three blocks from City Hall, with a continuous engagement with the City's Department of Licenses and Inspections that began in 2016 and runs through 2027. We know the City's systems, its procurement culture, and its infrastructure. We have shipped production software on the City's networks before, and we are ready to do it again — under a fixed-price contract, on a compressed schedule, with no subcontractors and no surprises.

---

## What We Are Proposing

TWC proposes to design, build, configure, deploy, and maintain a purpose-built special event permitting platform for OCRSE — a **fully custom, open-source-licensed solution** developed on our proven `civitas` civic application framework, deployed on existing City infrastructure, and delivered under a fixed-price contract with milestone-based payments.

Our solution directly addresses every mandatory functional requirement in Appendix A.1:

| Requirement | TWC Solution Capability |
|---|---|
| Multi-application hosting (A.1.1) | Configurable application registry supporting all eight OCRSE event types concurrently |
| Permit generation and distribution (A.1.2) | Automated PDF/digital permit issuance with audit trail |
| Service request creation (A.1.3) | Rule-based task generation routed to relevant City departments |
| Post-submission editing (A.1.4) | Workflow-controlled amendment portal with version history |
| Conditional logic workflows (A.1.5) | Field-level and section-level conditional logic; no irrelevant questions shown to applicants |
| Real-time status tracking (A.1.6) | Applicant-facing status dashboard and City staff queue views, updated on every workflow transition |
| External communication (A.1.7) | Threaded messaging with full retained history; exportable for records requests |
| Internal collaboration (A.1.8) | Interdepartmental task assignments, comment threads, and @-mentions |
| Centralized dashboards and calendars (A.1.9) | Unified event calendar and application pipeline views by department and event type |
| Third-party integrations (A.1.10) | REST API and webhook architecture; documented integration paths for payment, GIS, and business licensing systems |
| Invoice management (A.1.11) | Configurable fee schedules with invoice generation, tracking, and status |
| Multi-user access and permissions (A.1.12) | Role-based access control with department-scoped permission sets |

The platform will be delivered as **City-owned source code**, fully documented, MIT-licensed, and deposited in escrow at no cost to the City — consistent with our founding principle that public money produces public code.

## Why TWC for This Procurement

### 1. We Have Done This Work, in This City, on These Systems

Our decade-long engagement with the Department of Licenses and Inspections — migrating an 85,000-license legacy ColdFusion/Oracle stack to a modern Django/PostgreSQL portal — is the most directly comparable project in this RFP's evaluation criteria. We know the City's network environment, its identity and authentication landscape, its GIS stack, and its business licensing databases. We are not estimating integration complexity. We have already solved most of it.

### 2. We Can Meet the Timeline

We understand what is at stake. A system that is not live before FIFA Group Stage matches begin is not a partial success — it is a failure. Our proposed implementation schedule targets a **production go-live by May 15, 2026**, aligned with the City's anticipated project start date, with a 30-day reliability period completed prior to peak FIFA event activity. Our project team is already staffed, already local, and already partially familiar with the City's technical environment. We are not building a team to staff this project. We have one.

### 3. We Are Philadelphia

TWC is a Pennsylvania-incorporated, City of Philadelphia–certified M/W/DSBE, Small Diverse Business, and worker-owned cooperative. Our office is at 1234 Market Street. Every staff member who will work on this engagement lives and works in the Philadelphia region. We pay Philadelphia wage tax. We attend client working sessions in person. When OCRSE staff have a question at 7:00 AM the week of the All-Star Game, they will reach a TWC member, not a helpdesk in another time zone.

This is directly responsive to the City's Local Business Entity preference under Executive Order 04-12 and its commitments under the PHL Open for Business initiative. Awarding this contract to TWC is itself an act consistent with Mayor Parker's vision for an economically inclusive Philadelphia.

### 4. We Are Structured to Protect the City's Interests

Our worker-cooperative governance model means there is no private equity investor, no acquisition target, and no growth-at-all-costs pressure influencing how we staff or deliver this project. Our 100% client renewal rate for engagements over six months reflects what that stability produces in practice. We have shipped on fixed-price contracts for more than 80% of our procurement-required engagements without change orders. We understand the City's 20% retainage structure and have priced accordingly. We will not return at Month 4 seeking a change order because we underbid to win.

### 5. Our Open-Source Approach Eliminates Long-Term Lock-In

Every line of production code TWC writes belongs to the City from the moment it is committed. Our `civitas` framework is already MIT-licensed and in use by 14 municipalities. The City's staff can hire any qualified developer in the future to maintain, extend, or replace any component. There is no proprietary black box, no escrow negotiation, and no vendor leverage over the City's data or workflows. This is what durable digital public infrastructure looks like.
