### Agency Mission & Operational Scale

OCRSE is the central hub for permitting, licensing, insurance, and coordination of City services for public events in Philadelphia. The office:
- Manages approximately **1,700 special event applications annually**
- Coordinates across multiple City departments for each event
- Oversees eight (8) distinct event types, each requiring different levels of review and interdepartmental coordination
- Serves as the single point of contact for event organizers navigating multiple City agencies

---

### Why This RFP Is Happening Now: Strategic Context

**1. Major Global Events in 2026 Creating Urgency**
Philadelphia is set to host two marquee global events in 2026 that will dramatically increase the volume of special event permit applications:
- **FIFA World Cup 2026**: Philadelphia will host six matches (five Group Stage matches in June and a Round of 16 match on July 4th). The city is also planning the Lemon Hill Fan Fest (expected ~15,000 daily attendees) and a free FIFA Fan Festival celebrating community, culture, and sport.
- **MLB All-Star Game 2026**: Events run July 10–14 at Citizens Bank Park and the Pennsylvania Convention Center, culminating in the All-Star Game on July 14 broadcast on FOX.

These events are expected to trigger a surge in event permit requests that the current paper-based process cannot efficiently absorb.

**2. PHL Open for Business Executive Order 10-2024**
On April 15, 2024, Mayor Cherelle L. Parker signed **Executive Order 10-2024 ("PHL Open for Business")**, committing the Parker Administration to:
- Removing regulatory burdens
- Accelerating approval timelines
- Streamlining City permit and licensing processes
- Making Philadelphia more business-friendly for businesses of all sizes and in all neighborhoods

This RFP is explicitly identified as a **strategic goal under this Executive Order**, making it a politically visible and administration-priority project—not a routine IT upgrade.

The Order also renamed the Office of Business Services as the **Mayor's Business Action Team** (housed in the Department of Commerce), requiring quarterly reporting on service improvements.

**3. Current State: A Broken Paper Process**
The existing special event application is an **8-page PDF** with the following problems:
- All applicants must complete the same form regardless of event type, forcing many to answer irrelevant questions
- Eight distinct event types require different levels of information—none are dynamically filtered
- Some event types require submitting additional forms to other City departments (creating fragmentation)
- Completed PDFs are emailed to a general inbox (no tracking, no automation, no workflow)
- No real-time status updates for applicants
- No centralized internal workflow or task management across departments

### What the City Wants: Solution Requirements Summary

The City seeks a **COTS or configurable permitting software solution** that delivers:
- **Conditional logic workflows** that adapt to event type and applicant inputs
- **Multi-application hosting** and concurrent application management
- **Real-time status tracking** for both applicants and City staff
- **Centralized dashboards and calendar views** across departments
- **Cross-departmental internal collaboration tools**
- **External communication tools** with retained message history
- **Payment processing integration**
- **GIS, business licensing database, and third-party integrations** (via API, SSO, webhooks, file exchange)
- **Permit generation and distribution** directly through the system
- **Robust reporting and KPI dashboards**
- **Multi-user access with configurable permission levels**
- Installed on **existing City equipment and networks** (no hardware procurement under this contract)

### Competitive Market Context

The special event permitting software market has established COTS vendors already serving municipalities at scale:
- **Eproval** is noted as a market leader, deployed in 100+ municipalities including Bellevue, Denver, San Diego, and Seattle (which launched Eproval in 2025)
- The broader government permitting market is trending toward cloud-based COTS solutions offering predictable annual subscription pricing, faster implementation, and built-in ongoing upgrades
- Cities are favoring platforms with real-time permit status visibility, cross-departmental collaboration, and repeat-applicant features

Bidders competing against established players like Eproval should be prepared to differentiate on **configurability for Philadelphia's unique multi-department workflow**, **integration depth with existing City systems**, and **implementation speed** given the tight timeline ahead of FIFA 2026.

### Key Compliance & Local Considerations for Bidders

1. **Campaign Finance Disclosure (Chapter 17-1400):** Applicants must fully disclose political contributions to Philadelphia local candidates and political committees. Excessive contributions can disqualify a bidder.
2. **Local Business Entity (LBE) Preference:** Mayor's Executive Order 04-12 creates a preference for certified Local Business Entities or firms demonstrating local economic impact. Free LBE certification is available via the City.
3. **Equal Benefits Ordinance (Chapter 17-1900):** If contract exceeds $250,000, contractor must extend equal benefits to domestic partners of employees who are Philadelphia residents or City wage tax payers.
4. **Philadelphia Tax Compliance:** Applicants must certify tax compliance or arrange to come into compliance before award.
5. **LGBTQ Data Collection:** Voluntary LGBTQ business identification form required as part of submission.
6. **AI Use Disclosure:** Proposals must certify that any AI-assisted content was reviewed by a human subject matter expert and that the applicant owns or is licensed to use all submitted content.
7. **Transparency in Business Demographic Data:** For contracts valued at or over $94,000, demographic workforce data must be disclosed prior to contract conformance.
8. **20% Retainage:** The City withholds 20% of each payment until final acceptance of all deliverables.
9. **Source Code Escrow:** Proprietary software must have source code deposited in escrow at no cost to the City, with regular updates.
10. **Minimum 5-Year Maintenance Commitment:** Vendors must guarantee maintenance and support availability for at least five years from final acceptance.
11. **WCAG 2.1 AA Accessibility:** All web-facing components must meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 AA.
12. **Cyber Liability Insurance:** Minimum $1M per claim/aggregate required, covering data breaches, unauthorized access, and cyber-attack business interruption.

### Summary Assessment for Bidders

This is a **high-visibility, politically-driven procurement** tied directly to Mayor Parker's flagship business reform initiative and the City's preparations for two globally significant events in 2026 (FIFA World Cup and MLB All-Star Game). The agency has a clear, urgent pain point (an 8-page PDF process managing 1,700+ permits/year), strong executive sponsorship, and a compressed timeline. The City is open to COTS, configurable, or hybrid solutions. Winning vendors will need to demonstrate prior government permitting implementations, strong cross-departmental workflow capabilities, a credible implementation timeline achievable by mid-2026, and willingness to navigate Philadelphia's robust compliance and disclosure requirements.
